2. PC semantic obligation

The canonical PC helper +0x20 is a copy-and-control-flow hook, not a Boolean validator. It directly handles accepted one-byte and two-byte forms and otherwise falls back to the displaced original PC path.

Relevant accepted forms include:
- single-byte 0xA1..0xDF;
- two-byte leads 0x81..0x9F and 0xE0..0xFC;
- trails 0x40..0x7E and 0x80..0xFC.

The semantic obligation for Switch is therefore not merely range acceptance. Accepted compact single-byte and normal two-byte game-code data must survive all relevant upstream copy/normalization paths and reach downstream rendering/consumers without unintended Japanese halfwidth transformation.

3. Switch renderer/decode evidence

The existing Switch per-character decode family around mapped 0x445C60 was surveyed through its direct caller set.

- once a compact Korean byte reaches this renderer/decode family, this layer does not itself reject it as invalid.

This confirms the previously known decoder fact but does not establish family-level native equivalence.

4. Upstream copy/normalization evidence

A distinct upstream generic text parsing / normalization path exists before rendering. In its default Japanese halfwidth handling, bytes in the halfwidth-kana domain are normalized rather than guaranteed raw preservation. In particular, the 0xA6..0xDF subset overlaps compact Korean single-byte values used by the PC Korean patch.

The representative generic parser/wrapper family is broad rather than a narrow special-case path:
- direct call-site census: 1,078.

Separate UI/text-object paths also expose per-slot conversion controls. Observed slot patterns include main-string conversion OFF with auxiliary-string conversion ON, and alternating OFF/ON slot pairs. This is structural evidence that legitimate Japanese auxiliary/yomi normalization must remain distinguishable from Korean main-text raw preservation.

Therefore a global disable of halfwidth normalization is not an acceptable solution.
